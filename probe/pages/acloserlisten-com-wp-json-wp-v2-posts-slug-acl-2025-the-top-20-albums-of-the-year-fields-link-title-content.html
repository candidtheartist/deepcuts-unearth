<\/a>This year’s top album was a revelation: one of the humblest records we’ve ever covered drew acclaim from our entire staff. \u00a0In a year of tumult, it comes as no surprise that we would be drawn to the quiet and unassuming pleasures of an ordinary day.<\/p>\n

Our overall list also conveys a concern with the state of the world as it is. \u00a0Two albums capture the sounds of street protests in different nations. \u00a0Others address marginalized people in the U.S., erased histories in Mexico, the invasion of Ukraine and the genocide in Gaza. \u00a0On our larger list, multiple albums highlight climate change and governmental denial. \u00a0In 2025, music seems more relevant than ever before. \u00a0To be aware is to be energized, to be energized engaged.<\/p>\n

And so, two prevailing forces, comfort and anger, co-exist in our Top 20. \u00a0Each is essential. \u00a0In a harsh world, we need comfort, but we also need encouragement for the fight. \u00a0All this being said, there is always room for virtuosic music that impresses in its own right, as can be heard in a wide variety of timbres below, from Welsh primitive guitar to multitracked voice, retro electronics to European church bells. \u00a0We hope that you enjoy our selection of The Top 20 Albums of the Year!<\/em><\/p>\n

1) Okkyung Lee ~ just like any other day (\uc5b4\ub290\ub0a0): background music for your mundane activities<\/em><\/strong> (Shelter Press)
\nAs I write this, I start thinking I might be doing the music an injustice by solely listening to it. Focused upon it, I notice the album\u2019s diary-like structure, an invitation to distractedly fill in the blanks of pieces that tend toward repetition, clarity, and simplicity. As I calmly watch my cat sleep nearby, I start to think of how truly experimental <\/i>all of it is, because I\u2019m starting to feel like I am living through a moment, that the music yet shifts my attention away into the here and now, into a meditative kind of presence. The cat\u2019s belly lowers and rises, her fur standing out in a nice little pattern \u2013 the background becomes the moment\u2019s form, and I feel love. It is, after all, an infinitely common occurrence, each and every time decidedly unrepeatable, each and every time blurring into a mass of moments of which I haphazardly stumble both into and out of. I return to the album, and I notice it finished a long time ago. (David Murrieta <\/b>Flores)<\/strong><\/p>\n
