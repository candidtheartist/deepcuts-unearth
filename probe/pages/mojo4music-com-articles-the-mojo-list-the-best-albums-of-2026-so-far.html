The Best Albums Of 2026… So Far

MOJO's pick of the year's best records to date.

by MOJO |
Updated on1st October 2026 at 6.00pm
SUBSCRIBE TO OUR MAG
Follow Mojo on Google

We're now way past 2026's halfway mark, and it's clear we have yet vintage year for new albums. Alongside returning veterans and bona fide legends, there's been some remarkable leaps forward and unexpected changes of direction by some of the newer artists we've already grown to cherish here at MOJO. While it's just a tip of the iceberg when it comes to the deluge of great music being released every month - you'll need to pick up the latest issue of MOJO for that, or even better, grab a subscription in our 3 issues for £3 special offer - here is a selection of some of the albums we've really loved so far in 2026...

MOJO's Albums Of 2026... So Far:

Dry Cleaning - Secret Love

(4AD)

In a nutshell: Spotless third effort from Florence Shaw's post-punk seismographers.

What we said: "Dry Cleaning have lost none of their distinctive edge, their idiosyncratic set-up proving to be endlessly elastic, as big as they want, as small as they need,  to capture the chaos of the world... ★★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Lucinda Williams - World’s Gone Wrong

(HIGHWAY 20/THIRTY TIGERS)

In a nutshell: As she turns 73, Williams smells the sulphur of the apocalypse on her 16th LP.

What we said: "She's frail, wounded, and angry but she’s ready to tell it like it is... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Tyler Ballgame – For The First Time, Again

(ROUGH TRADE)

In a nutshell: Joy and heartbreak, rendered in analogue, from towering New England talent.

What we said: "Tyler Ballgame absolutely deserves acclaim right here and now... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Bill Callahan - My Days Of 58

(DRAG CITY)

In a nutshell: The dream river keeps on flowing through metaphysical singer-songwriter’s ninth post-Smog album.

What we said: "As unaffectedly beautiful as anything in Callahan’s catalogue... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Gorillaz - The Mountain

(KONG)

In a nutshell: Amorphous troupe’s ninth album features cameos from departed collaborators Dennis Hopper, Mark E. Smith and Bobby Womack.

What we said: "Gorillaz' most ambitious, and moving, record to date... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Shabaka - Of The Earth

(SHABAKA RECORDS/WARP)

In a nutshell: The sax is back! But Hutchings’s third solo LP is light years from jazz.

What we said: "Another ‘new age’ in Shabaka’s story dawns... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Squeeze - Trixies

(BMG)

In a nutshell: Revived and newly recorded, Chris Difford and Glenn Tilbrook’s pre-fame project might be the best work of their career.

What we said: "Sits comfortably beside Squeeze’s finest works of the late ’70s. It might even be better... ★★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Johnny Blue Skies & The Dark Clouds - Mutiny After Midnight

(HIGH TOP MOUNTAIN/ATLANTIC)

In a nutshell: Sturgill Simpson supercharges his Johnny Blue Skies alias for disco-flecked call to arms.

What we said: "Simpson has landed one of the best ideas of his increasingly remarkable career... ★★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Sunn O))) - Sunn O)))

(SUB POP)

In a nutshell: Be-cloaked duo get back to nature on elemental tenth LP.

What we said: "Music you feel in your body, your gut, your skull, a sensation of constant sonic regeneration and psychoactive power - ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

The Lemon Twigs - Look For Your Mind!

(CAPTURED TRACKS)

In a nutshell: Retro-minded siblings resist the tyranny of modernity by building their own world of gently psychedelic rock’n’roll.

What we said: "They have the confidence to fully inhabit their colourful, analogue world of vintage guitars, bowl haircuts, toy town innocence while also allowing aspects of the wider one to come in here and there too... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Aldous Harding - Train On The Island

(4AD)

In a nutshell: New Zealand enigma still keeps you guessing on transporting fifth album.

What we said: "Harding remains a fascinating songwriting provocateur...★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

The Coral - 388

(RUN ON/MODERN SKY)

In a nutshell: Cosmic scousers make a dub-tactic return to their roots

What we said: "By reconnecting with their past, The Coral have found the essence of who they are now – and it's pretty magical - ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Boards Of Canada - Inferno

(WARP)

In a nutshell: Long-awaited fifth LP from the Scottish brothers who altered electronic music’s collective unconscious**.**

What we said: "The sense is of something undefined being invoked... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Paul McCartney - The Boys Of Dungeon Lane

(MPL/CAPITOL)

In a nutshell: Pop’s master craftsman looks back across his life with a remarkable openness.

What we said: "As he approaches his eighty-fourth birthday, Paul McCartney is smart enough to deploy his inevitable frailty in service of what he wishes to share... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Beth Orton - The Ground Above

(PARTISAN)

In a nutshell: '90s comedown queen continues current winning streak with expansive and elemental ninth album.

What we said: "Elegantly shores up Beth Orton’s creative resurgence... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Jack White - Frozen Charlotte

(THIRD MAN)

In a nutshell: Following the return to form of 2024's No Name, former White Stripe largely lets his guitar do the talking.

What we said: "If rock music is in danger of going into cardiac arrest, then Jack White has the keys to the defibrillator... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Madonna - Confessions II

(WARNERS)

In a nutshell: Madonna plugs back into her clubland roots with a record that finds its place in the world by looking back.

What we said: "For Madonna, the musical pull of the dance floor is not just about escape but catharsis, a way of releasing dark buried emotion... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Panda Bear And Sonic Boom - A ? Of When

(DOMINO)

In a nutshell: Animal Collective/Spacemen 3 cosmic seekers throw open their cabinet of musical curiosities on second collaborative LP.

What we said: "There’s a sense of the pair layering and padding their songs like they’re trying to insulate themselves from the dark world outside... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

Phoebe Bridgers - Lost Weekend

(DEAD OCEANS)

In a nutshell: Dislocated by grief and loss, acclaimed songwriter makes a welcome return on long-awaited third solo album.

What we said: "For a record so defined by absence, Lost Weekend proves, thankfully, that Bridgers is still very much here... ★★★★" Read MOJO's review in full

The Tubs - Hard Life

(MERGE)

In a nutshell: Welsh/Scottish folk rockers deliver brutal truths on superb third album.

What we said: “The Tubs are an exceptional band, casually delivering brutal truths in the most glorious way...  ★★★★★” Read MOJO's review in full

Beck - Ride Lonesome

(Beck - Ride Lonesome)

In a nutshell: Knocked down by heartbreak, Beck Hansen sinks into acoustic sadness to stunning effect.

What we said: "A stunning display of the alchemy of turning pain into sheer beauty... ★★★★★” Read MOJO's review in full

Neil Young & The Chrome Hearts - Second Song

(REPRISE)

In a nutshell: With the help of his latest band The Chrome Hearts, Young delivers a reflective, late career classic.

What we said: “The vibes are warm, reflective, not a little frayed around the edges, as Young’s commitment to spontaneity only becomes more militant with age - ★★★★” Read MOJO's review in full...
